The money app that tells you before.

Expense manager, reminder and tracker, built France-first and local-first.

Most apps record the money you've already spent. This one looks ahead: everything you owe goes on one timeline, the app warns you before a renewal or a price rise hits, and it shows a single safe-to-spend number. It works with no bank connection; bank sync is an optional extra.

This report covers the market, the full feature design, feasibility area by area, bank sync, the tech stack, an effort estimate, a phased roadmap, costs, compliance, risks and the five decisions to make next. It is built from eight research areas, including a France ⇄ Tunisia currency pass, each fact-checked against primary sources on the same day.

02 · Market

A crowded field with one gap: nobody warns you before money moves

US premium apps have settled at about $100/yr and are now competing on AI agents. In France, the local leaders moved toward B2B data and wealth tracking, and no well-designed, scaled budgeting app is left. A small French envelope app does exist (Finzee, 7 App Store ratings), so safe-to-spend alone is not a moat. The moat is a French obligation layer: contract lifecycles, termination timing, warranty clocks and frais réels, which global apps won't build.

03 · Product

Eight signature features

The ones marked moat are the features global competitors are least likely to copy. All of them run on a recurrence engine that turns past transactions and contracts into future obligations.

F1

Safe-to-spend everywhere

One number, calculated from your upcoming obligations and goals rather than your bank balance. It appears on the widget, a persistent Android notification, the iOS lock screen and a watch. It turns budgeting into a glance.

F2

Obligation radar

Bills, subscriptions, trials, annual renewals, price rises, warranty and return windows on one timeline. Each item has an action: pay, cancel (with a link to the legal termination page), claim, or keep. Tagline: "nothing renews behind your back".

F3

2-second capture, offline

One tap, natural-language entry ("14,50 resto split avec Sam"), on-device receipt OCR, and Android bank-notification capture. With no bank feed, capture speed decides whether the data is complete.

F4

Proof vault with legal clocks

Every receipt carries the dates the law attaches to it: the 2-year legal conformity guarantee, return windows, and 3-year tax-proof retention. "Show me what I can still claim" becomes a simple query.

F5

Frais-réels autopilot

A live, year-round comparison of frais réels against the 10% flat deduction, a kilometre log using the official scale tables (dated yearly parameters), and exports for the tax return and for employers' notes de frais.

F6

Household money, no awkward talks

Fair-share rules (by income), private spaces, automatic balances and settle-up. It combines a budget app and a Tricount-style group in one ledger.

F7

What-if sandbox

Drag a planned purchase onto the forecast and see the low point, the change to safe-to-spend and the delay to each goal before you buy.

F8

Only explainable insights

Every alert or score opens onto the exact transactions and the parameter (with its source) behind it. In personal finance, trust is the product. AI only ever proposes; you confirm.

All eight are Speculative product design. Their legal basis (warranty, retention, termination) is Verified on service-public.fr and Légifrance.

05 · Bank sync

Two paths, one adapter

You asked to keep this decision open. Research says you can, at almost no cost, provided every source of transactions goes through a single TransactionSource adapter from day one.

Path A: smart capture (core)

  • CSV / OFX / QIF import with saved per-bank profiles and a duplicate guard. Works for every French bank.
  • Android notification listener reads your banking app's payment alerts as they arrive, on the device only, opt-in.
  • Receipt OCR (ML Kit), the share sheet, natural-language entry.
  • iOS: a Shortcuts "Transaction" automation feeding an App Intent (Apple Pay only, and Apple Pay doesn't exist in Tunisia Verified secondary sources).
  • Tunisia: this path is the only path, since there is no open banking. Add PDF statements and cash accounts (see France ⇄ Tunisia).
  • Cost €0 · no regulation · works offline.

Path B: open banking (add-on)

  • GoCardless Bank Account Data is closed to new sign-ups. Don't build on it. Verified · closure date ~July 2025 Inferred
  • Personal: Enable Banking restricted mode on your own whitelisted accounts. Free, non-commercial, manual review. Verified
  • Product: quotes from Enable Banking, Powens, Bridge (owned by competitor Bankin'), Salt Edge Partner. Run under their licence, not your own AISP licence.
  • Entry cost estimated at €200–600/mo + per connection Inferred, low confidence. Only real quotes settle it.
  • Under PSD2 (since the 2023 change), consent lasts up to 180 days, but each bank sets its own maximum, so the app needs a re-consent reminder before valid_until. Verified EBA, Enable Banking API

Verdict: Path A is the product, and Path B is a premium convenience that pays for its own aggregator fees. When a bank feed arrives, it reconciles "pending" captures against "booked" transactions instead of replacing them. Inferred

Budgeting across two currencies

  • Budgets live in their own currency. A combined view converts at a planning rate you choose, so a TND budget doesn't "overspend" every time the rate moves.
  • Safe-to-spend is calculated per currency pocket. A TND shortfall can't hide behind a healthy EUR balance. The widget shows the pocket for the country you're in, which you choose; there's no location tracking.
  • Non-convertible dinars count only in the TND pocket ("TND, local only"), never in a EUR total or goal.
  • Price-rise alerts compare in the subscription's own currency, so a rate move never triggers a false "price increased".
  • Country rule packs: French tax and legal features (frais réels, warranty clocks, résiliation) apply only to French-scoped spending. The Tunisia pack starts empty on purpose: no invented rules.

Design Speculative, based on 07a/07c

Optimising a two-currency life

  • The real cost of every conversion and foreign card payment, as an amount and a %, plus an "FX & fees" budget category ("FX cost this year: …").
  • Your own conversion methods compared by median effective cost, using labels you type yourself; the app ships no preset providers.
  • Foreign-currency subscriptions listed with the FX cost they add up to over a year.
  • Stay summaries: spending per country in both currencies, cost per day, FX share.
  • Reconciliation nudges: "3 TND payments are still estimates; import your statement to finalise them."

All of this shows your own data back to you. The app never recommends where or how to exchange money, and never predicts rates. Speculative

The five decisions that unblock the build

  1. UI stack

    Flutter 3.47, pinned with FVM (move to 3.50 when it is stable), conditional on the Phase 0 spike; Expo is the fallback. Speculative

  2. MVP platform

    Android first: the notification listener only exists there, no Mac is needed, and sideloading avoids store review while you dogfood it. iOS reaches parity in v1. Inferred

  3. Sync architecture (hard to reverse, decide before v2)

    MVP: encrypted backup files only. v2: a server-arbiter PowerSync + Postgres on an EU host, with field-level encryption, roughly 80 h cheaper than E2EE. Keep full E2EE as a paid "vault" option. A data model with UUIDv7, soft deletes and owner/household IDs keeps every option open. BCT rates stay fetched on the device until the BCT agrees to their reuse. Speculative

  4. Bank sync: when and with whom

    Personal Enable Banking in v2, or sooner for your own use. For the product, put it behind premium, contracted only once the company exists, after ≥ 3 quotes. Never make it the core value. Verified constraints
